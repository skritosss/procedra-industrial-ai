# Procedra pilot outreach messages

Status: public-safe outreach drafts for starting controlled pilot conversations.

Use these messages to start a practical conversation, not to claim deployment, safety validation, certified compliance, paid traction, revenue, or measured productivity gains. Keep every outreach tied to one narrow pilot: one instruction family, approved non-confidential materials, named reviewers, and explicit evaluation criteria.

## Core positioning

Procedra is a controlled local-demo prototype for human-in-the-loop industrial AI. It helps turn a task, technical context, documents, sources, and optional video context into a structured instruction draft with source context, local verification items, expert-review questions, version history, checklist evidence, and audit traceability.

The pilot question is simple:

> Can this workflow make the first draft and review process clearer enough to justify a focused pilot on one approved instruction family?

## Audience: technologist / production engineer

Emphasize:

- structure and completeness of first drafts;
- local verification questions;
- machine settings, tolerances, permits, and responsible roles are not invented;
- review workflow and version history;
- adaptation to an approved internal instruction template.

Opening:

> I am trying to understand whether Procedra could help prepare structured first drafts of operating instructions while keeping exact settings, tolerances, permits, and responsible roles under expert control. I would value your feedback on one instruction family where drafting or updating instructions is currently time-consuming.

Good questions:

- Which sections are mandatory in your instruction format?
- What information must never be guessed by an AI system?
- Who reviews a draft before it can be used?
- Which source documents are authoritative?
- What would make an AI draft useful but still safe to review?

## Audience: safety / quality

Emphasize:

- review boundary;
- hazards, PPE, control points, emergency actions, and quality checks;
- audit trail and execution evidence;
- no legal/safety certification claim;
- local procedures and qualified review remain mandatory.

Opening:

> I am not trying to automate safety approval. I am exploring whether AI can help create a more structured draft that surfaces hazards, PPE, control points, emergency actions, and expert-review questions before a qualified person reviews it.

Good questions:

- Which mistakes in instruction drafts create the biggest review burden?
- Which safety or quality fields are often missing?
- What evidence should be preserved around review and trial execution?
- What would make this unacceptable for even a limited pilot?
- What data must be excluded from a demo environment?

## Audience: digital transformation / operations manager

Emphasize:

- document-heavy operational workflow;
- traceability from draft to review to execution evidence;
- narrow controlled pilot rather than broad deployment;
- evaluation criteria agreed before pilot;
- integration and production hardening are later-stage topics.

Opening:

> Procedra is currently a controlled local-demo prototype, not a production rollout. I am looking for feedback on whether a narrow pilot around one instruction family could clarify value before discussing integrations or deployment.

Good questions:

- Which instruction or checklist workflows consume the most coordination time?
- What would a low-risk pilot need to prove?
- Which stakeholders must be involved?
- What data-security boundaries would be mandatory?
- What output package would be useful after a pilot: PDF, source list, expert questions, review log, execution summary?

## Discovery call structure

Use this 20-30 minute structure:

1. Two-minute Procedra context and boundaries.
2. Five-minute current workflow: how instructions/checklists are prepared today.
3. Five-minute pain points: where drafting, review, approval, or updates break down.
4. Five-minute safe scenario selection: one instruction family and allowed materials.
5. Five-minute pilot criteria: what should be tested and what should not be claimed.
6. Two-minute next step: demo, feedback, or stop.

## Do not say

- "Production-ready."
- "Validated safety system."
- "Customer-deployed."
- "Paid pilot."
- "Reduces time by X%."
- "Certified compliance."
- "Replaces technologists, safety specialists, quality specialists, or approved procedures."
